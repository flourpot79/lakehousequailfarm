What Are Our Quail Saying? A Guide to Quail Sounds

By Lake House Quail Farm · 3 min read

People are often surprised by how quiet quail are. Next to a backyard full of chickens, a quail coop is practically peaceful, and most of their chatter blends right into the birdsong outside. But spend a little time with them and you'll notice they have a whole vocabulary of their own. Here's what we've learned to listen for.

Soft chirps, coos, and purrs

This is the everyday background music of a happy flock. Our hens make gentle, songbird-like sounds while they scratch around, settle into a dust bath, or come over to see what we're up to. When you hear these little murmurs, all is well in quail world.

High-pitched squeaks and cricket chirps

Quicker, higher chirps usually mean excitement. Treat time gets a lot of these! Quail also use them to keep track of each other, almost like checking in with the rest of the flock.

Grumbles and squawks

Quail can be little grumps. A short, cranky squawk usually means someone is guarding a favorite spot, like the dust bath or a cozy corner, and wants everyone else to back off. It sounds dramatic, but it's mostly just bird bickering.

The male mating call

This is the loudest sound you'll hear from coturnix quail. It isn't a crow like a chicken rooster's, but a sharp, repeated call that some people say sounds like "chi-ca-go!" Males usually start calling at around four to six weeks old. They do it to claim their territory, call to the hens, or announce that they've noticed something. If you have close neighbors, this is the one sound to plan around.

Sweet talk

Males have a softer side, too. When they're trying to win over a hen, they'll make low, coaxing clucks, often while offering her a tasty morsel (or pretending to). It's surprisingly charming to watch.

Listening to your flock

The best way to learn quail language is simply to spend time with your birds. After a while, you'll know their normal, happy hum, and you'll notice right away when something sounds different. That's one of the nicest parts of keeping a small flock: you really get to know each bird.
